2. Sensor and Data Accuracy

All flight data displayed by the App is derived from consumer-grade sensors and is provided on an "as-is" basis. Specifically:

  • Barometric altitude — derived from the watch's pressure sensor. Accuracy is affected by local weather conditions, temperature, sensor calibration, QNH/QFE settings, and the specific device hardware. Barometric altitude may drift over time and may not reflect true altitude above ground.
  • Vertical speed (vario) — calculated from barometric pressure or GPS altitude deltas. Vario readings may lag, spike, or be inaccurate due to sensor noise, filtering algorithms, turbulence, or device hardware limitations.
  • GPS position, speed, and track — dependent on satellite visibility, atmospheric conditions, device antenna quality, and the United States government's operation of the Global Positioning System. GPS altitude is inherently less accurate than barometric altitude. GPS signal may be lost without warning.
  • Compass heading — derived from magnetometer and accelerometer data. Readings may be affected by magnetic interference, device calibration state, proximity to metal objects, or rapid movement.
  • Flight detection — the automatic flight detection feature uses heuristics based on barometric pressure changes and GPS data. It may fail to detect takeoff, falsely detect takeoff, or fail to detect landing.
  • Glide ratio and distance calculations — derived from GPS data and subject to all GPS accuracy limitations.

You must cross-check all flight data against other instruments, visual references, and your own judgment. Do not rely solely on Riser for any safety-critical flight decision.

3. Live Tracking Limitations

The App offers optional live tracking integration with third-party services (LiveTrack24, SportsTrackLive). If you enable live tracking:

  • Live tracking is a convenience feature only and is not a substitute for a dedicated personal locator beacon (PLB), satellite communicator (e.g., Garmin inReach, SPOT), or any emergency location device.
